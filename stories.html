EF — Twizted Journey
Shared with permission

Well Tonya, this is just another of many things that we have in common. About 3 years ago my nephew ended his journey in life. He was 22 years old, a high school graduate, and enrolled in the Indianapolis institution of art. He was close to getting a degree from that institution. He had a job, was going to school, had his own apartment, and had a girlfriend. From the outside, everything looked okay. Everything looked like he was successful. Unfortunately, that wasn’t the case on the inside.

Things were going on within my nephew that his mother, my sister, didn’t know about. Even if we had known, I’m not sure if we could have done much to stop him, but we could have done something to maybe deter him.

What a lot of people don’t know about me is that, being a Marine, I have taken on a lot of responsibility and carried a lot on my shoulders throughout the years. Some of it has really taken its toll. I worked so many hours for 28 years in the restaurant business that I didn’t even know what I was really going through.

After I could not work any longer and went on Social Security disability, I had a chance for the first time in a long time to just stop and smell the roses, as they say. But what I saw in me wasn’t pleasant. I was suffering from PTSD and depression. I thank God that I was able to recognize something was wrong. I may not have known what, but something was wrong, and I reached out to the Veterans Administration for help.

I have received help and continue to receive it. I’m so thankful for those men and women who have helped me in my battle and my struggle with depression. What I understand now is that I only wish I would have known my nephew was suffering from the same type of thing on the inside. Had I known, I could have reached out and at least offered him some help and pointed him in the direction to get help.

I would encourage all of you to take a hard, long look at your siblings, your children, and your family. If you recognize signs of depression or PTSD, please do not leave anyone alone to battle this disease. It is very real, and it does destroy people. Reach out. Offer help. Offer yourself. Most of all, just reach out. Don’t allow someone to deal with this on their own. Don’t allow anyone to fight alone.

It wasn’t until after my nephew’s suicide that I realized these things. I wish I would have known he was suffering in silence on the inside, just as I was suffering in silence on the inside. I reached out and he didn’t. So please, people, do something to help others, because tomorrow is not promised to any of us. God bless you all.

— E.F.

JC
JC — Twizted Journey · Janell Coomer
Shared with permission

As I reflect on the last 6 years, I just want to say I have had ups and downs. I have lost many people that I love and one of the greatest friends, RUGER my dog. ❤️ But I have so many people to be grateful for in my life.

My family has grown in so many ways. This has been such a blessing to me. My kids and grandchildren save my life every single day just by living their life. My wonderful husband has been my rock for 21 years. Even when that meant tough love, and I know now that was harder on him than it was on me.

There are still a few relationships that I would love to see mended, but I know that’s out of my control. All I can do is be present and be prepared to do the work if and when they are ready.

My job at Goodwill and the people I work with have taught me so much over the last year. I’m so blessed to have these wonderful people in my life and to work for a company that truly cares for me as an individual.

Storms have come and will continue to come, but as long as I look up to the one that keeps me grounded, I will make it through. Today I celebrate 6 years of broken chains from heroin and meth addiction. I would never have been able to say that without God taking me through homelessness and jail, and for that I am forever grateful.
